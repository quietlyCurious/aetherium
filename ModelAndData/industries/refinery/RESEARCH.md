# Refinery (Aurelia & Ferrum) — research brief

**This process is fictional on purpose.** The "refinery" pack was made for a
hackathon with an invented process, so that nobody could lean on real
industry knowledge. The station names (Intake, Stabilization, Refinement,
Power Charge, Shaping…), the products (A, B, C) and the "Aetherium shard"
in SIT11 don't correspond to any real plant. Don't read petroleum refining
into it, and don't add real process chemistry to it.

That's why this brief has **no external sources**: there's nothing to cite.
The pack's physics is generic manufacturing flow: a serial line of stations
with throughput, work in process and buffers, control and stability signals,
quality metrics and a few instrument tags. This document writes down the
fiction as it exists in the data, so that `generate.py`, `SCENARIOS.md` and
any future detectors all describe the same process.

History: the pack was hand-built before the generic format (spec v2),
converted once by `ModelAndData/tools/convert_legacy_to_generic.py` (spec §12)
and is now regenerated by `generate.py`. The asset tree, property keys and
metadata, and all attention-item and work-item text are unchanged from that
conversion, because saved settings and asset sets are keyed by them.

## 1. Hierarchy

`refinery → line → station` (3 levels, 80 assets). The unit of operation
(`unitLevel`) is the **line**: each line gets a Now-strip tile.

| Refinery | Lines | Station sequence (the same on every line of that refinery) |
|---|---|---|
| **Aurelia** | A1–A6, all on Product A (target 120/min) | Intake → Stabilization → Refinement → Inspection → Buffer → Output |
| **Ferrum** | F1–F6: Product B (target 125/min); F4 on Product A (120/min) until its 13:50 changeover to Product C | Bulk Intake → Power Charge → Shaping → Transfer → Output |

The two refineries are deliberately different shapes: 6-station lines with
a buffer and an inspection gate against 5-station lines with neither. One
`line` type covers both (it's a container), which is why the validator warns
that `line` instances have different child types. `output` is the only
station type both refineries share.

Relationships: 54 `feeds_into` edges on the `process_flow` layer, one per
consecutive pair of stations inside a line. There are no edges between lines
or refineries: each line is an independent flow, and the refinery is only a
roll-up of its lines.

## 2. What each station does (in the fiction)

| Station | Role | Its own signals besides throughput |
|---|---|---|
| **Intake** (Aurelia) | Receives raw input and checks each batch | batch/input variability, input defect rate, input quality, input signal noise |
| **Stabilization** | Brings the stream to a steady pressure and temperature under closed-loop control | control effort, deviation, oscillation, stability score, settling time, pressure transmitter + local mechanical gauge, flow meter, temperature |
| **Refinement** | The value-adding step: raises purity; a regulating element trims it | purity %, control effort %, input characteristic, consistency, defect/rework/yield, process variability |
| **Inspection** | Automatic accept/reject gate | reject rate %, pass rate, first-pass yield, false accept/reject, measurement noise, calibration |
| **Buffer** | The only real store on any line (≈1,040 units at 100 %) | level %, inflow, outflow, time in buffer, turnover, capacity utilisation, saturation and starvation risk |
| **Bulk Intake** (Ferrum) | Receives and meters bulk input | batch/supply variability, input defect rate, input quality, material availability |
| **Power Charge** | Energises the material; a motor-driven charger with its own drive train | charge rate, controller output %, energy per unit, oscillation, vibration, bearing temperature, motor current |
| **Shaping** | Forms the charged material | consistency, defect rate, rework, process variability |
| **Transfer** | A gated conveyor handing product to Output | transfer (gate) rate, motor current, blocking and starvation time |
| **Output** | Final release point; what leaves here is the line's throughput | final defect rate, on-time output, order fulfilment, output consistency and quality |

Every station also carries `system_stress` (a 0–30 load/strain index) and
`throughput_per_min`.

## 3. How material flows (the physics the generator uses)

- **One pace per line.** Every station passes on what it receives, so all
  stations on a line share a slowly drifting line pace, plus their own
  small count noise and a fixed counter bias of a few tenths of a unit/min.
- **No storage except the Aurelia Buffer.** On a Ferrum line, and upstream
  of the Buffer on an Aurelia line, a station that slows or stops **starves
  the stations after it** and **blocks the ones before it** in the same
  5-minute sample. A Ferrum Transfer that jams stops the whole line (the
  operators put it in a controlled hold).
- **The Buffer absorbs.** Aurelia's Buffer level integrates inflow − outflow
  (`level += (in − out) · 5 min / 10.4 units per %`). A level controller trims
  the outflow by 0.3 units/min per % away from setpoint. An upstream shortfall
  drains the buffer before it reaches Output (SIT03); a downstream restriction
  fills it (SIT04).
- **Line throughput = what leaves the Output station** (`line_throughput`
  is the Output's `throughput_per_min`, sample for sample).
- **Instrument tags follow the process.** Motor current is proportional to
  station load (idling at about 20 % of normal current when stopped). The
  Stabilization flow meter reads that station's throughput, and the pressure
  transmitter and local gauge read the same true pressure through different
  taps. Inspection pass rate = 100 − reject rate, and first-pass yield =
  pass rate − a small rework share.
- **Quality moves downstream.** A purity drop at Refinement shows up as
  Inspection rejects about 10–15 minutes later, then as line scrap.

## 4. KPIs and how they roll up

Line KPIs are **rolling 24-hour** figures. The 22 hours before 08:00 are
assumed normal, so an incident during the shown window moves them by
lost-time ÷ 1,440 min. They don't bounce back when the incident ends, just
as a real rolling OEE doesn't.

| Key (line) | Definition |
|---|---|
| `line_availability` % | normal − 100 × (units lost while unplanned-down) ÷ (1,440 min × target) |
| `line_performance` % | min(100, pace ÷ target − 100 × (units lost while running slow) ÷ (1,440 × target)); catch-up above normal counts back |
| `line_quality_factor` % | 100 − normal scrap % − 100 × extra scrapped units ÷ (1,440 × target) |
| `line_oee` % | availability × performance × quality factor (declared as a `formula` derivation) |
| `line_throughput`, `line_target_rate` | Output's rate; the planned rate (0 during a planned changeover) |
| `line_scrap_rate` | the current 5-minute scrap %, normal plus scenario scrap |
| `total_wip` | work in process between stations in lots (the Buffer's contents aren't included) |
| `instability_index` | normal ≈ 1, rising with oscillation, cascades, stops and changeovers |
| `system_health_index` % | normal − 0.25 × excess instability − condition penalties (for example 0.55 per mm/s of excess drive vibration) |
| `flow_efficiency` | normal − 0.25 × WIP lots added in the last hour (material entering but not leaving) |

Lost units are counted against the no-incident flow for the same noise, so
noise alone never moves a KPI. A **planned** changeover (F4 from 13:50) is
excluded from availability and performance.

Refinery KPIs are declared derivations over the refinery's 6 lines at every
point: `refinery_throughput` and `refinery_target_rate` are sums,
`refinery_oee`, `_availability`, `_performance`, `_quality_factor` and
`_health_index` are means, and `refinery_instability_index` is the max.

**Headline numbers per type** (P1 in `properties.json`): refinery
throughput, OEE, health and instability; line throughput, OEE, scrap, WIP,
instability and health; Buffer level; Refinement purity; Power Charge
charge rate; Transfer rate; Inspection first-pass yield; Output on-time and
fulfilment rates.

## 5. Property families

- **Flow / WIP:** throughputs, transfer and flow rates, buffer level, flows
  and times, WIP, target rate.
- **Events / Losses:** defect, reject, rework, scrap rates; blocking and
  starvation time.
- **Stability:** control effort, controller output, oscillation, deviation,
  settling time, variability, instability, stress, signal noise, vibration.
- **Quality:** purity, yields, pass rate, consistency, quality scores,
  calibration, false accept/reject, material availability.
- **Derived Metric:** OEE and its factors, health index, buffer risk indices.
- **Condition:** bearing temperature, motor current, pressures, temperature,
  energy per unit.

Several types have more than the spec's ~8 properties (line 11,
stabilization 11, refinement 10, buffer 10, inspection 9, power_charge 9).
They're kept because saved displays are keyed by them.

## 6. Failure modes behind the scenarios (in the fiction)

| # | Failure mode | Asset | First symptom | Spreads to | Confirmed by | Fix | Timescale |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sluggish regulating element | Refinement | Throughput down while control effort climbs | Inspection starved, Buffer drains, upstream paces down | Field inspection of the regulator | Adjust the regulator | tens of minutes |
| 2 | Output capacity restriction | Output | Buffer level rising at steady inflow | Saturation risk, time in buffer; line throughput dips | Output inspection | Clear the restriction, pace upstream while draining | ~1 h |
| 3 | Drive bearing wear | Power Charge | Vibration rising at unchanged load | Bearing temperature, energy per unit, line health | Drive inspection / vibration route | Replace the bearing at a planned stop | hours to days |
| 4 | Loose transmitter connection | Stabilization | Pressure reading swings | Nothing: the gauge, flow and temperature stay steady | Compare with the local gauge | Reseat the connection | minutes |
| 5 | Purity drift at Refinement | Refinement → Inspection | Purity falls, rejects follow ~15 min later | Scrap, first-pass yield | Independent quality check | Adjust Refinement | < 1 h |
| 6 | Over-pacing beyond downstream capacity | Power Charge → Shaping | WIP and blocking rise while the gross rate looks fine | Defects, rework, scrap, flow efficiency | Compare gross against effective output | Cut Power Charge demand | ~1–2 h |
| 7 | Power-delivery interruption | Power Charge | Output collapses for a sample | Bulk Intake blocked, Shaping starved, Transfer empties then accumulates | Event order along the line | Reduce demand, repair the supply | minutes, ~40 min to recover |
| 8 | Manual over-correction | Power Charge control | Charge rate oscillation grows | Controller output, throughput swing, instability | Timing of manual moves against response lag | Return to the validated setting | ~30 min |
| 9 | Physical obstruction at the gate | Transfer | Rate → 0 with a motor current spike | Whole line in controlled hold; availability | Visual (a shard in the gate) | Validated block-clearing procedure | ~20 min |
| 10 | Recurring micro-stops | Power Charge | Short dips in rate, frequent | Transfer starvation, instability | Stop log, frequency against baseline | Inspect at next pause | builds over an hour |
| 11 | Input characteristic shift | Refinement | Input characteristic rises; purity trends toward limit | Rejects, output quality | Input lab result | Small validated Refinement adjustment | ~1–2 h |
| 12 | Planned changeover (not a failure) | Line | Target → 0, throughput runs down | Instability, health index | Changeover plan | — | ~30 min |
